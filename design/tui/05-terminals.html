Decisions

  • List on top, one mirror below. Terminals are a fleet, and a fleet needs an index; but you only ever read one at a time. A grid of tiny panes would give you eight unreadable panes instead of one readable one.
  • Idle interactive shells get their own group and their own clock. A root bash sitting at / for 2h 14m is not "running" in any useful sense — it is a liability. It turns amber at 30 minutes, and the group count is repeated in the header so it is visible from the session screen's F8 hint too. A forgotten root shell should embarrass someone.
  • Amber here means the same thing it means on F6: this one needs a human. It is never used for "in progress" — that is blue, and every running command wears it.
  • Exit codes are the sort key of the dead. Failed and succeeded are separate groups because you re-run one of them and ignore the other; a merged "finished" group would make the failure a scanning problem.
  • Destructive keys are two-step and asymmetric. k is SIGTERM, unshifted, no confirm — it is recoverable. ⇧K is SIGKILL, shifted, and still opens a confirm card. The shift key is the first half of the confirmation.
  • The mirror closes with a ledger line — └ exit 0 · 3.2s · finished 4s ago — the same └ shape that closes a turn in the transcript. One glyph, one meaning: "this thing is over, here is its total".
